input: resume, top third

Senior Software Engineer · Jan 2019 – Present
Led cross-functional initiatives and contributed to architectural decisions. Experienced with multiple frameworks and tools; passionate about clean code.

what the first pass caught

  • evidenceThree duty verbs, zero outcomes. "Led", "contributed", "experienced" say the candidate was present, not that anything changed.
  • speedStack, domain and seniority are not findable at a glance. Three facts a screener hunts for should be the three that land first.
6–8 s first pass: a reported heuristic, not a rulenamed filter per finding

input: one bullet

Responsible for managing the migration of legacy systems to cloud infrastructure for the department.

critique, in the candidate's own wording

  • evidenceManage the migration of what, from what, to what, with what result? Name the systems; a screener can't map vagueness onto their stack.
  • clarityStart with the verb, not the job description. "Migrated the department's [N] legacy services to [platform]" is the same claim, screened in half a second.
every fix shown on your wording"make this stronger" is not a finding

input: weak bullet

Improved application performance significantly, earning recognition from leadership.

impact-boost

Rewritten shape: "Cut checkout latency by [metric: ?] on the store's main flow." Then one question back: what did latency actually drop to?

  • risk"Earning recognition from leadership" is an unverifiable soft claim. It reads as padding and costs credibility on the bullets around it.
numbers come from youno metric, no guess: a question or a [metric: ?]

house rules

What the lens never does.

Invent nothing

Numbers, titles, employers and dates come from the candidate. A missing metric gets a question or a visible [metric: ?], never a guess.

Tend, not rule

Screening behavior is reported tendency, not insider knowledge. Region, industry and seniority change what applies, and the skill says when.

Stay honest

No claim that would collapse under interview follow-up. Gaps and career changes get a brief, honest context line, never concealment.
