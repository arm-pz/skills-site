## Final output

# Income blueprint: Xero bookkeeping retainers for micro-businesses

I used your profile and filled the gaps with assumptions, which I've marked. If any are wrong, tell me and I'll adjust.

## 1. Read of you
- £1,500 a month on about 8 hours a week is roughly £43 an hour (35 hours a month). That's realistic for a chartered accountant with Xero skills. It isn't realistic for content or digital products on that time, because those take months to build an audience.
- You hate being on camera, so this is a text-and-referral business: LinkedIn posts, direct messages and local introductions.
- £300 is enough for a services business because there's nothing to build. Compliance costs (section 8) may use a lot of it.

## 2. Recommended model
**A fixed-price monthly Xero bookkeeping and VAT-prep retainer for very small Manchester businesses.**

The sums (assumptions, so check them against local market rates):
- 5 clients at about £300 a month gives £1,500.
- At about 5–6 hours per client per month, that's 25–30 hours, which fits your 8 hours a week.

**Runner-up:** one-off Xero clean-ups and setups at a fixed fee, around £400–£900 each. This is also your entry offer, since it leads into the retainer.
**What would flip the choice:** if your job's hours make fixed monthly deadlines risky, go one-off only. You'd need roughly 2–3 projects a month instead.

## 3. Demand check (before building anything)
Have 5 conversations with owners of businesses with a turnover of £30–150k, such as tradespeople, salons, Etsy or Shopify sellers and small contractors. Ask how they handle their books and what it costs them when it goes wrong. Then offer a clean-up at a fixed price.

- **Evidence:** at least 2 of the 5 say they'd pay, or 1 pays a deposit.
- **A no:** 0 of 5 show interest, or they only want "someone cheap". In that case, change the niche before you change anything else.

## 4. Offer
- **Buyer:** a sole trader or small limited company in Greater Manchester with messy books, on Xero or ready to move to it.
- **Problem:** bank feeds are unreconciled, VAT and Making Tax Digital deadlines are stressful, and they don't know their real profit.
- **Outcome:** books reconciled monthly, VAT return ready to file, and a one-page profit summary.
- **Price:** £250–£350 a month, based on the cost of a missed deadline or an unhappy accountant, not your hours. Start with a clean-up for £400–£800 depending on the backlog. These numbers are assumptions.
- **Delivery:** you work in their Xero file asynchronously, with a 15-minute call or written note each month.
- **Guarantee:** if the first month's reconciliation isn't right, you fix it free.
- **Differentiator:** a chartered accountant's judgment at bookkeeper prices, with no camera and no meetings.

## 5. Audience and channel
- **Primary:** LinkedIn, text only, plus local referrals from mortgage brokers, business-bank contacts, other small practices that don't do bookkeeping, and Facebook groups for local tradespeople.
- **Messaging angle:** "Know your real numbers, never miss a deadline."

## 6. Content plan
- **Pillars:** common Xero mistakes, UK tax and VAT deadlines in plain English, and anonymised before-and-after clean-ups.
- **Cadence:** 2 text posts a week, about 30 minutes each.
- **Example hooks:**
  1. "3 Xero bank-rec mistakes that quietly wreck your VAT return."
  2. "If your accountant only calls you in January, read this."
  3. "What 'unreconciled' really costs a small business."

## 7. Sales path
1. Send a short direct message: "Hi [name], I help [trade] owners in Manchester get their Xero books clean and VAT-ready. Would a free 10-minute look at your setup be useful?"
2. Do a quick free review, then offer the fixed-price clean-up.
3. If they're happy, offer the retainer, with the first month at the retainer rate.
4. Follow up after 3 days and again after 10, then stop.
5. Ask every happy client for one introduction.

## 8. Automations and tools
- **Automate later:** invoicing and payment reminders, and a scheduled monthly checklist. Don't automate anything until you've done it manually for a couple of clients.
- **Tools:** Xero (their own subscription, or practice access), a free scheduling tool for calls, and a simple engagement letter template. Check current pricing and free tiers for all of these.
- **Ignore:** a website, a CRM and ads.

**Compliance, which could use part of your £300:**
- **Anti-money laundering:** offering bookkeeping for a fee generally means registering with an AML supervisor. If you're an ICAEW member, you may be covered through them.
- **Professional indemnity insurance:** get quotes.
- **ICO data protection registration:** check whether you need it and what it costs.
- **Your employment contract:** read the outside-work and conflict-of-interest clauses, and check whether you need to tell your employer.
- **Your professional body's rules** on practising privately.

Check current requirements and costs with ICAEW, HMRC and the ICO. I haven't verified them this session.

## 9. 7-day plan (about 9 hours total, with a little over a day to spare)
| Day | Outcome | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Check your contract and your professional body's rules on private work. Get PI insurance quotes. | 1 |
| 2 | Write a one-page offer with a fixed-price clean-up and a retainer. | 1 |

| 4 | Send 10 personalised messages and publish your first post. | 1.5 |
| 5 | Follow up and book 2–3 conversations. | 1 |
| 6 | Hold the conversations and do free setup reviews. | 2 |
| 7 | Send quotes, draft an engagement letter and review results against the evidence threshold. | 1 |

## 10. START HERE FIRST
In the next hour, read the outside-work and conflict-of-interest clauses in your employment contract, then ask your professional body whether you can offer bookkeeping privately and what AML registration you need. If you're clear on both, everything else can go ahead.

Two things would sharpen this: do you have any existing contacts who run small businesses, and what would count as "working" in 30 days: a first payment, or a few firm leads?
