# Study system: a 20-minute conversation in European Portuguese (EP)

## 1. Goal and assumptions

**Outcome (provable):** In about mid-May 2027, I hold a recorded 20-minute unscripted conversation with my partner's family on everyday and family topics. I get through it in EP, I follow what they say when they speak naturally (they may slow down or repeat), and I repair breakdowns in Portuguese instead of switching to English or Spanish.

**Assumptions (correct me; each one changes the plan):**
- Today is 2026-11-13, so there are about 26 weeks. At 5 h/week that is about 130 h *if every week happens*. If you average 3.5 h, it is about 90 h. This is arithmetic on your numbers, not a prediction of fluency.
- "Decent Spanish" is a self-report. The week-1 diagnostic tests it. It is not yet a finding.
- The family speaks EP, not Brazilian. Their dialect, speed and willingness to slow down are `unknown`. Ask your partner.
- Beginner in Portuguese: no evidence of prior study.
- You have not told me why you quit apps after two weeks. I assume it was some mix of no feedback, no stakes and boredom. This system is built against those three.

**Whether the goal is reachable in 6 months:** `unknown`. It depends on your real hours, the quality of live feedback, and how generous the family is as listeners. The week-1 diagnostic and the week-6 gate (below) are what make it knowable.

## 2. Anti-quit design (read this first)

Your history says the plan's biggest risk is week 3, not the grammar. So:

1. **Stakes are booked, not willed.** In week 1 you book and prepay a recurring weekly 60-minute live session with a tutor or exchange partner. This is a dependency you must arrange. A person waiting for you at a fixed time is the strongest anti-quit device here, and no app provides one. A prepaid slot also costs you money to skip.
2. **A floor day.** On a bad day the whole obligation is 10 minutes of review cards. A floor day counts and keeps the habit alive. Skipping two floor days in a row triggers the restart rule below.
3. **Restart rule, decided now:** after any gap of 3+ days, do not "catch up". Do the floor day, then resume the schedule at the current week. Never re-do missed sessions.
4. **No streaks.** Log two separate columns: *sessions done* and *gates passed*. A streak can run while you learn nothing. A gate can't.
5. **Family as a pull.** Ask your partner to arrange a 5-minute call in Portuguese with one relative around week 6, and another around week 14. These are dependencies on other people. Treat them as bonus feedback, not as the plan's backbone.
6. **Variety by design.** Each week has four different activity types, so no single one is the thing you get bored of.

## 3. Component map

**Minimum viable set** (without these, a conversation can't happen at all):

| Component | Kind | Proof it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EP sound system: vowel reduction, nasal vowels, sibilants. This is where EP is least like Spanish. | Procedural | A tutor or recording check confirms you are understood on 10 given words without repeating |
| ~800 highest-frequency words and set phrases for family/home/food/work/travel | Declarative | You use each correctly in a new sentence, without Spanish leakage |
| Core present-tense verbs, plus the tu / você / title-and-name address choices | Declarative + judgement | You pick the right address form for each family member, and say why |
| Past tense and near-future forms, to talk about "what I did / what we'll do" | Procedural | A 3-minute spoken account of last weekend with no stops over 5 seconds |
| Understanding natural-speed EP | Procedural | You summarise a 2-minute native clip correctly, without the transcript |
| Repair moves: "can you repeat that / say it slower / what does X mean / how do you say Y?" | Recovery | You use them in Portuguese, on cue, in live conversation |
| Environment: a recording setup, a card deck, the booked live slot, a log file | Environment | You can start a session from cold in under 2 minutes |

**Transfer you can reuse (labelled as reuse, not new learning):** Spanish vocabulary and much of the grammar frame. The risk is false friends and Spanish "autopilot" in speech, so the pronunciation and error log targets those.

**Deferred** (with the trigger to pick it up):
- Personal infinitive and finer clitic placement: when your tutor corrects the same slip twice.
- Subjunctive beyond common set phrases: when you can't follow the family saying "I hope that…" / "if only…".
- Reading and writing fluency: when you want to text the family. It isn't needed for the gate.
- Regional accent training: when you know where they are from and can't follow them.

## 5. Practice routine (about 5 h/week)

| Block | Per week | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live conversation | 1 × 60 min | The booked slot. Whole-part-whole: attempt the stage's gate task, drill the part that broke, redo it. Ask the tutor to note recurring errors in writing. |
| Speaking drills | 2 × 45 min | Record yourself on the stage's task. Replay it against a checklist: sounds on your hit list, Spanish leakage, filler length, repair moves used. Re-record the worst minute. |
| Listening | 2 × 45 min | EP-only audio at a level where you catch about 60–70% (adjust by feel). Shadow 1–2 minutes aloud, then check against the transcript afterwards. |
| Review cards | 5 × 10 min | See section 6. Also your floor day. |

Total is about 4 h 50 min.

**Feedback you can reach alone:** a recording replayed against a checklist; a transcript diffed against what you caught; your own earlier recordings (compare week 1 with week 5). **Feedback that needs a person:** the live tutor session. It is the only check on whether you are understood, so protect that slot above all others.

**Moving the band:** two clean sessions in a row means raise difficulty (faster audio, no notes, a longer task). Two failures on the same error type means drop to the component under it and drill that. Repeating the same task with no new errors is comfort, so change the topic.

**Error log (one line per session):** attempted → where it broke → my stated cause → next target. Do this at the end of every block, and write the next session's target before you stop.

## 6. Review schedule

Review items (cards): one fact or one decision each, phrased as a question with a single checkable answer. Include EP-specific false friends and your logged errors. A card you can't answer without the source open is a comprehension gap, so move it to section 8 instead of re-reviewing it.

**Starting ladder (a default, not a constant):** same or next day, then 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 4 weeks, 8 weeks.

**Adjustment rule:**
- Correct with no hesitation → multiply the interval by about 2–3.
- Correct after a struggle → hold the interval.
- Wrong, or confidently wrong → reset to the start of the ladder and re-learn it before the next attempt.

**Load cap:** the 10-minute daily slot. If due cards exceed it, cut new cards or lengthen intervals. Don't let a queue build, since review debt is the common way people drop these routines. Add about 10 new cards a day at most, and start with fewer.

Speaking tasks follow the same ladder as repeat tasks: re-do a stage 1 task in stage 3 to check it held.

## 7. Exercises (from your own context)

1. **Family map (week 2):** write and record a description of each person you'll meet: who they are, what they do, how you address them. Done when the tutor confirms the address forms and you can say all of it without notes.
2. **The visit (week 8):** plan out loud a day with the family: what you'll eat, do, and say. Done when you can talk for 2 minutes without a pause over 5 seconds.
3. **Retell (every 2 weeks from week 13):** summarise a native-speed clip in Portuguese, then compare with the transcript. Done when you hit the main points and can name what you missed.
4. **Repair drill (week 10):** have the tutor speak fast or vaguely on purpose. Done when you use a repair phrase in Portuguese every time instead of nodding.
5. **Dress rehearsal (weeks 22–25):** two timed 20-minute recorded conversations. Done when you've reviewed the recordings against the checklist and logged three fixes.

## 8. Open gaps (things to test, not volunteer-assumed)

- **Listening, not speaking, is likely your real bottleneck.** Spanish speakers often read EP easily but find spoken EP hard to follow: it is fast, with many reduced or swallowed vowels. Test: the week-1 diagnostic should include a 2-minute native clip. If you catch under about half, shift 1 drill block per week to listening.
- **Spanish autopilot.** Spanish may leak into pronunciation and word choice more than English does, and sounds correct to you. Only the recording checklist and the tutor can catch this.
- **Most resources and many tutors are Brazilian Portuguese.** Make sure the tutor and audio are EP; check the audio source before you rely on it. `[unverified]`: how many apps you've used before default to Brazilian.
- **Unknown:** why you quit apps. If it's "got boring" the variety design helps. If it's "no time", the floor day does. If it's "felt I wasn't improving", the gate log matters most. Write one line on this in week 1.
- **Unknown:** whether the family will slow down, speak English with you, or both. This changes what "20 minutes" demands. Ask your partner.
